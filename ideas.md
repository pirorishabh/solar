# SolarGrid — Design Direction

## Three potential approaches

### Grid Atlas
**Very Brief Intro:** A quiet, precision-first energy command center built from dark field-survey surfaces, technical labels, and wide operational vistas. It feels considered, engineered, and suitable for teams running real renewable assets.

**Probability:** 0.07

### Bright Yield
**Very Brief Intro:** A sunlit, editorial analytics environment that uses warm mineral paper, soft green data signals, and generous breathing room. It makes utility information feel legible and optimistic.

**Probability:** 0.04

### Kilowatt Noir
**Very Brief Intro:** A nocturnal, high-contrast control room with acidic signal color, dense data marks, and cinematic energy infrastructure imagery. It makes the operating state feel immediate without becoming a neon cliché.

**Probability:** 0.09

## Chosen approach — Grid Atlas

**Design Movement:** Operational modernism with Swiss wayfinding discipline and a restrained industrial-editorial finish.

**Core Principles:**
1. Treat data as field intelligence: information sits in layered planes, not generic cards.
2. Create hierarchy through size, contrast, and carefully placed signal color rather than decoration.
3. Keep the interface calm under pressure with visible status, clear labels, and generous negative space.
4. Let actual energy infrastructure imagery provide texture where a chart alone would feel anonymous.

**Color Philosophy:** The base is a near-black blue graphite that evokes early-morning operations rather than a generic software dark mode. Soft smoke surfaces separate working regions, while a single electric chartreuse signal color serves as an owned operational highlight: live generation, selected controls, and confirmed system health. Alert states use measured amber and coral so urgency does not compete with core navigation.

**Layout Paradigm:** The application behaves like an operations map: a fixed vertical utility rail anchors the left edge, a compact control bar spans the top, and the content flows through an asymmetric field of a dominant live-production panel, a vertical power-flow spine, and satellite intelligence modules. Mobile collapses to a horizontal utility dock while preserving the order of operational priority.

**Signature Elements:**
1. Fine lime locator lines and small square status pips that connect module headers to live states.
2. Rounded-rect surfaces with one intentionally chamfered corner to echo photovoltaic cell geometry.
3. Subtle graph-paper micro-grids, technical coordinate labels, and faint hairline dividers.

**Interaction Philosophy:** Interactions are direct and stateful. Navigation feels like moving between instruments; active states are unmistakable, secondary actions defer visually, and every button acknowledges input with a crisp scale response. Nonfunctional integration controls clearly identify themselves as previews rather than pretending to operate live infrastructure.

**Animation:** Use short 140–220ms opacity and transform transitions with a strong ease-out. Modules should enter in a restrained 40ms cascade, hover states lift by one pixel and brighten their locator line, and the live-flow SVG uses a slow, low-key dash movement. All non-essential movement is disabled for reduced-motion preferences.

**Typography System:** Space Grotesk is the display and navigation face: compact, engineered, and confident. IBM Plex Mono carries measurements, timestamps, and labels in uppercase tracking. The hierarchy moves from 10–11px mono micro-labels, to 14px utility labels, to 22–30px operational headings, to a 48px overview reading at desktop. Avoid Inter throughout.

**Brand Essence:** SolarGrid is the focused operating layer for teams who need to see, understand, and act on distributed solar performance without noise. **Precise, calm, field-ready.**

**Brand Voice:** Headlines are concise and observational; CTAs use concrete verbs, while microcopy states the condition and time plainly. Example lines: “The field is producing ahead of plan.” and “Trace today’s conversion losses.” Generic welcome language and vague starter phrases are prohibited.

**Wordmark & Logo:** The mark is a chartreuse solar core captured inside three graphite photovoltaic bars, creating a simple energy-cell silhouette. The wordmark uses a customized letter-spaced Space Grotesk treatment, with the small inner counter of the “o” acting as the sun core.

**Signature Brand Color:** **Signal Lime — #D8FF3E**.

## Style Decisions

- On desktop, the branded energy-cell mark, letter-spaced SolarGrid wordmark, and sticky utility rail remain fully visible as the operating-layer anchor.
- Every route should read as an operations map: a dominant intelligence plane is surrounded by supporting modules, while locator lines and status pips make state relationships visible.
- Signal Lime is reserved for selected navigation, healthy/live state, decisive numerals, and locator geometry; amber and coral remain exclusive to watch and critical conditions.
- The meter-data route is an operating instrument named **Metering**, never a generic feature placeholder; its demo disclosure remains calm, specific, and research-aware.
- The SolarGrid wordmark uses a visible solar-core dot and tight engineered spacing, while every major plane repeats a photovoltaic chamfer with locator corners or square status geometry.
